H84 · Current model review · October 25, 2026 · Owner: the overseer · Independent reviewer: the builder

The model learns what might happen. Its current planner is mostly trying to copy an old body pose.

That is the main disconnect from your goal: a body that learns to get closer to something it wants, reach it, grasp it and keep hold of it.

Wellness is the computer's score for how well the body is doing. It includes touch comfort, ability to move and how accurately the model predicted its senses.

The parts named wellness, imagination, intention and forward model exist. But several connections needed to make them work together are missing, switched off or bypassed. We should have checked that complete chain before treating another small reaching repair as the route to the larger goal.

Update, 7:00 PM: The new code passed 14 checks with a small test model. It examined predicted moves without changing what the model had learned. What you expected—and what the code currently does

INTENDED GENERAL LEARNING LOOP

1. NoticeSee the surroundings and feel the body.
2. Imagine sequencesPredict what several possible sets of moves would cause.
3. Choose consequencesPrefer the sequence expected to improve the body's well-being or useful experience.
4. Act and learnMake the first move, observe the result, correct the predictions and choose again.

CURRENT BODY-PLANNING ROUTE

1. RememberPick a recent moment that scored well compared with the body's recent average.
2. Copy its poseUse that moment's body positions and turns as the goal.
3. Judge one movePredict the body after holding one command for a quarter-second. Keep a changed command only if its predicted pose beats the first proposal and the actual starting pose.
4. Move and repeatExecute through joint controls, observe what happened and learn from it.

The missing connection: the active body planner does not choose a sequence because of the useful consequences expected later. Choosing a good past moment is different from judging the future results of a proposed sequence.

Example only: shifting the body first might briefly move the hand away from a ball, but make the next reach possible. A rule that demands an immediately closer hand can reject that first move. The model should discover the useful sequence; we should not prescribe “shift, then reach, then close fingers.”

Where the connections fail—and one risk to holding

1. “Wellness drive” does not predict the result of a move

Only a fixed body-position penalty supplies this input to the controller. The measured touch, physical capacity and prediction-accuracy terms supply no direction for changing the action. The input can push away from the penalty's position boundary; it cannot answer “will this move improve comfort later?”

The term is named motor_state. It measures body-point positions relative to their centre, not actual joint-angle limits. wellness.py:310–393; lem_model.py:3343–3451, 4358. Its slope is an input, not a learning signal for future benefit. The separate future-wellness learning branch at 3459–3467 is off.

2. The body score leaves future wellness out

The active search tries to reduce the predicted difference from the remembered pose. Even enabling the existing future-wellness option would not repair this route: its physical-goal optimization bypasses that term.

intention_generator.py:270–287, 609–627. The comparison also requires improvement within the quarter-second window.

3. Later imagined moves use an old body position

The prediction loop advances its sensory estimates, but carries the starting physical pose forward unchanged. A second wrist-relative command can therefore be interpreted from where the wrist started, rather than where the first move put it. The current planner limits itself to one move to avoid relying on that error.

predictive_state.py:173–174, 246–253; lem_model.py:6993–7012.

4. Practicing predictions is not choosing a sequence

Imagination practices predicting three successive moments using movements that were actually performed. That improves a prediction skill. It does not, by itself, compare different possible sequences and choose the one with the best later result.

live_test.py:247–304; lem_model.py:3497–3581. Three prediction steps span 0.15 seconds of simulated time here.

5. The original plan also needs a correction

The original specification's “multi-step” example chooses the next move toward the same goal, then repeats. It never compares the total consequences of competing sequences. Restoring that example alone would leave this weakness.

UEMA specification 3.6, §10.3, lines 534–547. Its §7.4 also omits the connection from a hypothetical command to its future physical consequences.

6. Learned future-condition forecasts do not choose

The persistent-memory model learns to forecast bodily needs. The self-state model also learns future body condition. Their forecast outputs are not used by the active command chooser. Persistent memory still helps the forward model; the missing connection is specifically from those future-condition forecasts to the action decision.

lem_model.py:4301–4318, 4841–4851; self-choice off.

A holding risk in the goal score

The goal score emphasizes how each signal differs from its recent average. If a good condition stays steady, its difference from that average shrinks. That could make maintaining a useful condition stop standing out. The formula confirms this risk; it has not yet shown whether the body abandons a real grasp for that reason.

wellness_channels.py:380, 448–483; lem_model.py:9191. Repair review must distinguish “getting better” from “staying well.”

A separate problem with the ball example

The current caregiver ball can be made to follow the hand by code after contact. That is useful assisted experience, but it cannot tell us whether finger forces actually lifted and held the ball. Free-moving objects already exist in the environment; genuine grasping must be judged on physical support.

caregiver.py:428–465; active option: toy-hand-off follow. Keep assisted records labelled as assisted.

What this means for the repair

Keep the learned model and repair how it uses its predictions to choose. This does not call for a programmed list of toddler actions or a reward for “grasp the ball.”

  1. Make imagined body changes usable by the next imagined move. The second move must start from the first move's predicted result. Joint geometry can keep a pose consistent; the learned model still has to predict motion, contact and support.
  2. Connect predicted consequences to the existing wellness system. Candidate actions must change predicted future senses and bodily condition, and those changes must reach the score used to choose. A detached measurement of the current pose cannot supply that connection.
  3. Compare sequences, not just successive single moves. Judge a first move partly by what it enables afterward. Execute only the first part, observe again and revise the rest.
  4. Teach and judge the same actions that reach the body. Include joint limits, hand-relative coordinates, smoothing, command duration and reflex interventions. Do not compare an imagined command with a differently executed one.

Why would it want the ball? Recognizing or predicting a ball is not enough. The learner needs experience connecting interaction with things to its own needs, curiosity or control. Those learned connections must influence imagined choices. We should not insert a special “ball reward” to fill that gap.

Also required: keep useful conditions valuable after the initial improvement, and preserve opportunities to discover new actions. A score that forgets the value of continued holding, or a schedule that crowds out exploration, would undermine the repaired planner.

First implementation decision: reuse the existing prediction loop and sequence chooser where their interfaces fit. Review the exact replacement for stale physical state and the shared consequence score before enabling either. The current short pose-continuity package does not repair these connections.

First decisive check: demonstrate that a candidate's first action changes a later predicted consequence and that this later consequence can change which first action is chosen. Then compare predictions with real outcomes during preserved-state learning. A simple artificial example can check the computation; only physical behavior can establish the ability.

Evidence, uncertainty and protection of existing learning

Examined source: D:/UEMA_wt/persistent_goal_20261025, commit 2d7718add67e7ad8a461a6f7cbbccf5cee69e6f6. Completed-run receipt: target_unblended_496250_u_a2, endpoint 496256. Pending command: persistent_goal_20261025/package_pg/command_pg.json. The pending goal-persistence change is not a completed training result.

Search size is not movement distance: the ordinary optimizer can change each eligible position coordinate by up to 1.80 cm and rotation by up to 11° relative to its first proposal. Those bounds do not limit how far the body can physically move. The enabled stored-target candidate is outside that search box. It still must beat the first proposal's forecast and the actual starting pose. “First proposal” does not mean “do nothing.” See physical_goal.py:54–59, 291–298, 325–363.

Two shared learning objectives: lem_model.py:3327–3332 constructs a same-step reconstruction target; 3611–3619 constructs the delayed next-step target; 3871–3899 includes both in the loss. Their coexistence is verified. Whether they compete enough to impair useful forecasts remains a measurement question. Read existing loss/gradient records before changing their weights.

The completed receipt lists about 18.1 million parameters and the six sensory/action streams above. Its recorded command at step 496250 came from the planner. This audit inspected records and source without loading or modifying the learned weights.

What remains usable: observed transitions, sensory prediction training, physical-command lessons and preserved checkpoints. Narrow pose improvements remain narrow pose results. Assisted ball-following records remain assisted experience. No percentage of “lost training” can be inferred from these code findings.

What needs further learning: a repaired future-state interface and any newly connected consequence forecast must be trained and checked on actual outcomes. Existing experience should be reused where its inputs and targets still match. Preserve the eight worlds and full optimizer/model state; do not restart the learner to simplify the comparison.

What this review does not claim: one-step replanning can produce longer behavior when each local move helps. Exploration can discover new poses. Visual vectors can represent objects without named slots. None of those facts supplies the missing general comparison of delayed consequences. Likewise, a free root joint permits translation but does not demonstrate successful crawling under these actuators and contacts.

Evidence that will settle the repair: the same candidate action must affect later forecasts, later wellness must affect the chosen first action, and the observed physical outcomes must improve under the resulting choices. Keep the external ability measurement separate from the model's own training objective.

Adversarial review: what must not slip through again
  1. Trace a complete path from a candidate action to its later physical consequences, to wellness, to the chosen command. A module name is not evidence that the connection exists.
  2. Check the exact live configuration and the executed command. Distinguish trained, used for choice, shadow-only and switched-off modules.
  3. Advance the entire state needed by the next move, including body geometry, command duration and relevant memory. Never reuse the starting pose as a future pose.
  4. Include a sequence whose useful first move temporarily worsens the immediate pose score. Explain how the selector can choose it.
  5. Keep candidate forecasts comparable with the actual body: units, coordinate frames, joint constraints, smoothing and interventions must agree.
  6. Challenge the wellness objective itself: can stillness, repetitive touch or another useless loop score well? A better predictor alone cannot correct a poor objective.
  7. Judge useful, repeatable behavior with real contact physics. Do not substitute a lower prediction error, a drawn pose or caregiver assistance.
  8. Preserve learned state and identify which old observations remain valid. Make runtime and temporary-file costs explicit before execution.
