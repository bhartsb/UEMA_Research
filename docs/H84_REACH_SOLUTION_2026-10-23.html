Reaching a target pose: the straight path

The task in one line. The body is at pose S. An earlier saved pose T (the target record) is the target. A command is sent to the motors every 250 ms. After each command, measure how far each hand is from where it is in T. Make that distance go to zero and stay there.

The measure, used everywhere on this page. D = the straight-line distance, in cm, from a hand's hand point (a fixed point inside the palm; the recorder's name for it is the palm point) to the same point in T. One D per hand per command, read three times: at the start of the command, as the body head (the part of UEMA that predicts where each body part will be) predicted it would be at the end, and as measured at the end. That is the three-way comparison. Arrived = both hands' D at or below 2.0 cm at the end of a command. One hand within 2.0 cm is reported beside it. The 2.0 cm is a choice; its basis: the hand's collision box in the body model is 3.6 cm across and 3.8 cm long (half-widths 1.8 cm and 1.9 cm), so 2.0 cm is the longer half-width rounded up.

Words. A model step is one 50 ms update of UEMA; a command is held for 5 model steps (the command hold, flag --motor-hold 5). The direct command is the target pose T sent to the motors as a command; the ceiling is the measured D that the direct command reaches. The plan's parts are called stages so that "step" keeps one meaning.

Between two planner commands, a different command moves the hand with no direction

The planner sends every second command. 2 • The five reasons it has not worked

Each reason is a measured fact. Each is removed by one of the stages in section 3.

  1. Half the commands are not chosen for the target. Every second 250 ms command is the motor controller's command with noise, and it displaces the hand a median 1.69 cm with no net direction (section 1). Removed by stage 2.
  2. A planner command can describe a body that cannot exist. Each of its 26 rows (25 body parts and the eyes) is a pose for one part on its own, and the rows can contradict each other. In the screenshot's command (run POSE_A_off_494025, body 3, model step 494,810) the left forearm's requested direction needed an elbow angle 57.8° beyond the elbow's range (the right forearm's, 14.4°), and the two knees 76.4° and 75.5°. The solve that turns the rows into motor controls then outputs a compromise that reaches none of the requests. Removed by stage 3.
  3. The command is chosen by a predictor whose predicted results are wrong. The search scores each candidate by the pose-error score: the body head's predicted positions and turns of the 25 body parts against T, where 0 means every part at T and 1 means every position axis one typical 250 ms movement (1.80 cm) off with the turns exact. In the run of October 23 the predicted score was below the measured one by a median 0.634 over the 66 commands the search changed (0.57 over all 72), and the measured left hand ended farther from T than its predicted D in 38 of 72 commands, the right in 41 of 72. The search picks whatever this body head scores lowest. Removed by stage 4.
  4. Even a command that describes the target exactly may be physically slow, and how slow has been measured on one start only. On October 21 (run seg41j, body 5, model step 483,120, a different command from the screenshot's), with every arm and leg joint given its target angle as its command, the arm joints closed 20 of 100 of their angle gap by 250 ms and 28 of 100 by 550 ms; over the first 250 ms the chest's holding load (the torque needed to hold the pose against gravity and contact, divided by its motors' maximum torque) was 1.81 (0.11 over the whole second); opposing pulls between parts were flagged. A September 25 test on other starts read 49 to 66 of 100 of a reachable angle gap closed in 250 ms, with the motors at their control limit (the ±1 rail of the motor control) in 77.6 of 100 records. The two readings disagree, so the number of commands a reach needs is unknown. Measured by stage 1.
  5. The planner has never been compared with the simplest control. The simplest control is the direct command: send T itself, keep it, and measure the hand. Its measured D is the reading a planner is compared against (a sequence of different poses could do better than one kept pose; stage 2 has a branch for that). Without it, "44 of 72 nearer" cannot be judged against anything. Measured by stage 1.
Why it has been hard, in one sentence. The system searches 234 numbers that need not describe a real body, scores them with a body head whose predicted results are wrong by a median 0.634, sends the winner for 250 ms, lets a command not chosen for T take the next 250 ms, and has never measured what the body does when simply told where to go.
3 • The six stages, in sequence

Build the replay tool and measure how well the starts rebuild. Measure the ceiling. Give the planner every command. Make impossible commands and wrong predictions impossible. Judge against the ceiling.

Every stage states what runs, on what input, what is measured (with the scale's two ends), the line, what happens on each outcome, the cost on a measured basis, and who decides. No training runs before stage 2. Each stage produces the three-way table of section 1, so progress is read the same way every time. Every registered check obeys the project's rule of October 21 (AGENTS.md, "Execution cost and closure"): at most 900 s of executable time per named question, retries included.

Stage 0. Build the replay tool, and measure how well the saved starts rebuild. This is the only thing that can start tomorrow.

Why a build comes first
The one replay tool that exists, physical_comparison.py (branch repair/whole-body-request-2026-10-21, folder docs/gain_of_function_v4/planner_recovery/whole_body_request_2026-10-21/, 2,013 lines; the commit holding its code is 466867a0f, the branch head 2e4a5e52c adds only a pins file), rebuilds one body's world from a saved state by replaying the recorded motor controls, then sends a command of its own and records the result. Its run, body, model file, start state and model steps are constants in the file (run seg41j, body 5, model steps 481,800 to 483,120). It cannot be pointed at another run without code changes, so every measurement below waits on this build.
What the recording holds, and what the saved start is
The recorder writes one file per 5 model steps per body (a chunk). For the replay the chunk holds ctrl: the 90 motor controls the body received at each of the 5 model steps, after the trainer's blending of each new command with the previous one, stored as 16-bit floats; and the joint positions per model step (stored as quantized differences). It does not hold the 26 × 9 command. So the replay feeds the saved 90 motor controls model step by model step. The saved start is the preserved checkpoint D:/UEMA_Research/external/D/UEMA_preserved/s4r_measure_S4R_measure_496000/run/checkpoints/ckpt_step_496250 (model, optimizer and runtime state) and its companion, the eight worlds' physics state, which that folder's episode_state.json points to: D:/UEMA_Research/external/D/UEMA_scratch/h84/d1_planner_recovery/attempts/s4r_measure_496000_a1/evidence/recording/checkpoint_state/S4R_measure_496000/ckpt_step_496250_f3b30525a71743f78cd4073be0093361.pt.gz (619,853,771 bytes, sha256 c59f9eb0...). Both must be present and hash-checked before the tool runs; the companion sits on a scratch drive and is the first thing to copy under a preserved name.
What is built
A version of the tool that takes: a companion (the eight worlds' state); a recording folder; a list of planning steps per body; and a command to send at each. Mode 1 (physics only): rebuild the 8 worlds once, replay the saved motor controls model step by model step, branch a copy of each world at each planning step, and from each branch send the test command for 40 model steps. Mode 2 (with the learner): load the saved model beside the worlds and run its forward pass at each branch point, so that the body head's inputs at that start (the transformer's output and the achieved pose) are recorded; stage 4 needs them. Outputs per attempt: rebuilt_starts.json (per body, per planning step: the three mismatch readings below), outcomes.json (per start, per variant, per command: both hands' D, the 25-part mean distance, the motors at their control limit, the holding loads, the contacts), and the three-way table as CSV. Branch: repair/reach-ceiling-replay-2026-10-24 (proposed name). Owner: the builder builds it; an independent review agent reads the whole diff before any row runs, as for every package here; the first registered row is reach_ceiling_precheck_496250 (proposed id), its commit recorded in the row. Cost of the build: code and review; no measured basis for its duration.
The pre-check (the first registered row)
Input: the companion and checkpoint above and the October 23 run's recording (D:/UEMA_Research/external/D/UEMA_preserved/s4r_pose_learning_S4R_pose_learning_a2_496250/run/observer/world). Rebuild the 8 worlds and replay the saved motor controls to each of the 9 planning steps per body (8 × 90 = 720 model steps). At each of the 72 starts, compare the rebuilt body with the recorded one by the October 21 tool's three readings: the mean angle between the rebuilt and recorded orientations of the 15 body parts that tool scores (degrees; 0 = identical), whether the contact pairs are the same set, and the largest distance between a rebuilt and a recorded contact place (cm).
The line and the outcomes
This row has no pass line, and that is deliberate: the October 21 lines (1.554° and 0.0924 cm) were one body's achieved values on one replay of 1,319 model steps, and the registry says of that replay "whether it does is unmeasured" for other records; whether this recording rebuilds within those lines is unknown. The row reports the three readings for all 72 starts. The operator then sets the mismatch a stage 1 start may have (section 4, decision 1), and starts above it are excluded from stage 1 and listed. Known candidates for a mismatch, to be named in the report with their measured share: the 16-bit rounding of the saved motor controls; a physics difference between the recording process and the replay process (the October 21 replay found the contact pairs changing between consecutive records in 1,052 of its 1,319 model steps, so contacts are the likely place).
Cost, measured basis
The October 21 tool replayed 1,319 model steps and ran ten test commands of 20 model steps (1,519 model steps in all) in 218.3 s of wall time and 2.7 GiB, in one process; it wrote 3.0 MB of evidence (the limb-control attempt, 720 KB). The pre-check replays 720 model steps and sends nothing: 103 s by the same arithmetic, in one row. It runs on the processor with the trainer paused: the tool runs with the graphics card off and 1 thread (the October 21 row's setting), and the memory guard admits a heavy job only beside a paused trainer.
Who decides
The overseer authorizes the row. The operator sets the mismatch allowance after it (decision 1).

Stage 1. Measure the ceiling: send the target pose itself, keep it, measure the hand. No learning.

What runs
Stage 0's tool, mode 1, on the run of October 23: one replay per body from 496,250 to its last planning step (at most 90 model steps), branching at each of the 9 planning steps whose rebuilt start is within the mismatch allowance (at most 72 starts, each with its target record T).
The direct command
For each of the 25 body parts: x, y, z = that part's recorded world position in T, in cm; the 4 turn numbers = that part's recorded world orientation in T, as the quaternion x, y, z, w the recorder stores; the radius = the start's recorded command's radius (no motor reads it); the strength = 1.0 in variant A (the largest strength a command accepts) and 0.5 in variant B (the level of the planner's own commands: the three left-arm rows of the screenshot's command carried 0.44 to 0.49). The eyes' row = the start's recorded eye row. The same T is kept for 8 consecutive commands (40 model steps, 2 s) with no other command in between.
What is measured
After each of the 8 commands: D for each hand (cm; 0 = at T); the mean distance of the 25 body parts to T (cm); for each motor, the count of the 40 model steps at which it was at its control limit (0 to 40); for each of the 11 part groups the October 21 tool reports (left and right arm, thigh, calf and foot; lower trunk; chest; head), its holding load over the 40 model steps (0 = none; 1 = the motors' maximum); the floor contacts. Output: the three-way table with the predicted column empty, and one curve of D against command number per start and variant.
The line
None; a measurement. Its readings, for each variant: (a) of the starts run, how many arrived (both hands at or below 2.0 cm at the end of the 8th command) and how many had one hand within 2.0 cm at the end of any of the 8 commands; (b) the median D per hand after 1, 2, 4 and 8 commands. The ceiling for a start is the better of the two variants' D after 8 commands.
What happens next, judged per start and then counted
Each start that did not arrive in either variant is put in one of two classes: over capacity if any part group's holding load is above 1.0 over the 40 model steps or any of that group's motors is at its control limit in more than 20 of the 40 model steps (a motor at its limit more than half the time cannot follow its command); under-driven otherwise. Then: (i) Arrived on at least half the starts run (36 of 72 when all 72 run; below half the ceiling itself is too low for a planner to be compared against it): the body reaches T under the direct command; these curves are the ceiling; go to stage 2. (ii) Fewer than half arrived and the over-capacity class is at least as large as the under-driven class: T is not reachable from S for those starts with these motors; the next work is a filter on the target choice (only targets whose holding loads at T, computed from the saved pose, are within capacity), a configuration change to the planner goal selection and a hand-written rule about targets, which section 4 lists as a decision; the under-driven starts are then re-read under (iii). (iii) Fewer than half arrived and the under-driven class is the larger: the same replay on the same starts with the motor gain raised, flag --decoder-kp from 6.0 (the run's value) to 8.0 (the flag's own documented stable value: "8 is stable once armature is on") and to 12.0 (twice the run's value). If a gain reaches half, that gain is the configuration for every later stage (a decision in section 4: the body head was trained at 6.0 and must be retrained or re-checked at the new gain in stage 4). If no gain reaches half, the under-driven starts that still do not arrive are an open finding (neither load nor gain explains them) and are reported as such, with no rule attached.
Cost, measured basis
8 replays of at most 90 model steps plus 72 starts × 2 variants × 40 model steps = 6,480 model steps. At the October 21 rate (1,519 model steps in 218.3 s) that is 931 s in one process, above the 900 s rule, so it runs as 2 rows of 4 bodies each, 466 s per row by the same arithmetic, one after the other. The gain sweep of (iii) is the same cost per gain value. Evidence written: on the scale of the October 21 attempts (3.0 MB and 720 KB) per row. Processor only, trainer paused, as in stage 0.
Who decides
The overseer authorizes the rows. The operator decides the arrival distance (2.0 cm) and the number of commands kept (8).

Stage 2. Give the planner every command.

What runs
A bounded training run of 100 model steps from the preserved state at 496,350 (D:/UEMA_Research/external/D/UEMA_preserved/s4r_pose_learning_S4R_pose_learning_a2_496250/run/checkpoints/ckpt_step_496350, with its companion), on the code of the run of October 23 (commit 7dd0eabda, branch repair/s4r-pose-fields-s1-2026-10-22, which has the planning-capture flag --planner-capture on that run used) or on the pair's commit 71841320b, with the limits and stop rules of the pair authorized on October 23: 100 model steps; 600 s per run including 180 s for saving; stop lines of the body's position error above 5.3464 cm or its turn error above 33.1384° at a command's end, a non-finite loss or gradient, or invalid geometry; the adoption monitor's stop lines, which read the parent runs' stats files (the pilot of October 23 stopped at 496,425 after 75 model steps because a disk relocation had removed those files, so they are checked present before launch). Two configuration changes. First, the planner search runs for every command: --intention-every 5 instead of 10, with the command hold at 5 model steps as now. By the trainer's own rule (train_mimo.py, the exec_src tally near line 2142: with the hold equal to the planning interval every executed command is the planner's) this also means no babble noise reaches the body; the run's record must show the executed babble fraction at 0. Second, the target is kept: --goal-locality on. That flag does two things (lem_model.py, _gl_select, line 2065): it keeps a target until it is reached, left behind or 200 model steps old, and it restricts the choice of target to ring entries within the model's own measured 14-step drift distance. Its "reached" and "left behind" lines are distances in token space (the running one-step drift and the 14-step drift), not centimetres, so the cm readings below come from the records as in section 1.
What is measured
The three-way table per command; the change of D between consecutive commands (section 1's in-between reading), which must read 0.0 cm on every pair because there is no in-between command; and, after the run, stage 0's tool (mode 1) on this run's own captured starts (rebuilt from the state at 496,350 and this run's recording), so the ceiling and the planner are read on the same starts and targets.
The line
Construction: executed babble fraction 0 and between-command change 0.0 cm on every pair; otherwise the run is INVALID (the configuration did not take), is fixed and rerun. Reading: the median D per hand after 1, 2, 4 and 8 commands at a kept target, beside the ceiling on the same starts. A run of 100 model steps gives at most 20 commands per body; if fewer than 8 bodies have a stretch of 8 commands at one target, the reading is taken at the longest length all 8 bodies reach and is labelled with that length.
What happens next
With M = the planner's median D after the read length and C = the ceiling's on the same starts: M above C by more than 1.0 cm: the gap is a number and stages 3 and 4 must close it. M within 1.0 cm of C either way: stages 3 and 4 are not needed for this task; go to stage 5. M below C by more than 1.0 cm: the direct command is not the best command for this body, the planner's curves become the reference instead of the ceiling, and the finding is reported before anything else runs. The 1.0 cm is stage 5's adoption margin, stated there.
Cost, measured basis
The run of October 23 (100 model steps, 8 worlds) took 421.9 s of trainer time and 75.7 s to save; the same bounds apply. The search running twice as often adds its cost per decision, which the run itself measures. Stage 0's tool on the run's starts costs what stage 1 measured. A new training configuration needs its own preflight record (the seven gates, with the bounded-exception reasoning the authorized pair carries).
Who decides
The overseer, as for every launch. The operator decides whether this run goes before or after the pair's replacement attempt.

Stage 4. Make the body head right about the hand, and make an impossible prediction impossible.

What runs, first
Stage 0's tool in mode 2 on the stage 1 starts, so that each direct command's record holds the body head's inputs at the start beside the measured outcome: 72 starts × 8 commands × 2 variants = 1,152 records of commands that describe a real body. Then train the existing body head on them. Split by body, because one body's records share one target: bodies 0 to 3 train (576 records), bodies 4 and 5 stop the training (288 records), bodies 6 and 7 are the test set (288 records). Loss: the body head's existing loss term (the difference between its predicted 250 ms movement of each part and the measured one, in the 1.80 cm and 11.0° units it uses today). Optimizer: the trainer's own, AdamW (lem_model.py line 1871) with its two groups (weight decay on tensors of two or more dimensions, none on the rest), learning rate 0.0003 and weight decay 0.01 (Source/Python/lem/config/defaults.py lines 390 and 391), unless the row reads other values from the run's configuration and states them. Batch 8; 72 updates per pass; up to 20 passes; training stops when the stop set's error has not fallen for 2 passes. Nothing else in UEMA is trained; the saved model is read, the trained body head is written beside it, never over it.
What is measured
(a) On the test set (bodies 6 and 7, direct commands): the predicted hand point's distance from the measured hand point at the end (cm; 0 = exact), per hand, and the predicted D against the measured D. (b) On the planner's own recorded commands of the same starts (stage 3's replay gives their measured outcomes, mode 2 their inputs): the same two readings, for today's body head and for the retrained one. Today's body head's reading (b) is computable now from the run of October 23's saved prediction arrays against its end records and has not been computed; the one case drawn so far (body 5, model step 496,280) read 7.49 cm for the right hand, the largest disagreement of the run, not the median.
The line
(a) median at or below 1.0 cm on the test set; (b) median at or below 2.0 cm on the recorded planner commands of the test bodies; and in each set the predicted D below the measured D in no more than half the commands. The three numbers are choices; their basis: 1.0 cm is half the arrival distance, 2.0 cm is the arrival distance, half is the count at which the predicted D is below the measured D as often as above it.
What happens next
(a) and (b) met: the body head ranks commands to within the arrival distance; the pair's replacement attempt (one run with a geometry loss term off, one with it on) becomes the experiment that keeps it that way, and stage 5 follows. (a) met, (b) not: the body head is right about commands that describe a real body and wrong about the search's commands, which points to stage 3b. (a) not met: the body head cannot learn this from 576 records; the repair that needs new code is stage 4b: the body head outputs joint-angle changes and the pose comes from forward kinematics (a forward kinematics in torch exists today only for the fingers), so a prediction can never place the elbow off the upper arm.
Cost, measured basis
Mode 2's replay is stage 1's cost plus the model's forward pass at 72 starts (unmeasured; the trainer's own rate was 100 model steps in 421.9 s with learning, so 72 forward passes without learning are inside one row). The only measured figure for the training is 2.93 ms for one re-forward and backward of the body head on synthetic inputs (the loss-term check of October 23); it is not a training step on real records. At most 20 passes × 72 updates = 1,440 updates; 1,440 × 2.93 ms = 4.2 s of model time, so even at 100 times that cost the row is inside the 900 s rule. Reading (b) for today's body head is a processor job on saved arrays with no model run.
Who decides
The overseer authorizes the training as a registered row with its own preflight record. The operator decides the 1.0 cm and 2.0 cm lines.

Stage 5. Judge every planner against the ceiling, on the dashboard, every run.

What runs
Two code changes. In the trainer: the keep rule below, which replaces the keep rule of October 22 (Source/Python/lem/model/physical_goal.py, function pose_return_keep, lines 54 to 59 at commit f0183150b: a changed command is sent only if its predicted score is below both the start's and the first command's). On the dashboard: the card below. Both are code and review with no measured basis for their duration; both need an independent review; the trainer change needs a preflight record before any run uses it.
The keep rule
For every hand whose start D is above 2.0 cm, the command's predicted D for that hand must be below the start's D; a command that meets this for every such hand is sent; a command that fails it for any such hand is not, and the fallback command is sent instead. The fallback command is the operator's choice (section 4): the first command (today's rule), or the direct command (a hand-written pose-to-command rule inside the running system).
The adoption rule
A planner configuration is adopted only when, on the same starts and the same kept targets, (a) for at least half the starts its measured D for both hands after 8 commands is within 1.0 cm of the direct command's, and (b) its arrived count is at least the direct command's minus 2. Both numbers are choices for the operator to set before the comparison runs; 1.0 cm is half the arrival distance.
The dashboard card
For every run: per command, per hand, the three columns of section 1 (start, predicted, measured) and a fourth, the direct command's measured D on the same start, which exists for every run stage 0's tool has been run on; the arrived count and the one-hand-within-2.0 cm count; the between-commands change. The card replaces "nearer in N of M" as the way progress is stated.
Who decides
The overseer for the code and its launch; the operator for the fallback, the 1.0 cm margin and the 2-arrival margin.
What this plan does not need. No new predictor before stage 1 has answered whether the body can arrive at all. No arm-fit check inside the search (the fitter built October 22 stays a diagnosis tool). No new goal selection beyond the keeping of stage 2 and, in outcome (ii) of stage 1, a load filter that section 4 lists as a decision. Stage 1's direct command is a measurement control sent in replays of saved states; whether it also becomes the runtime fallback is the operator's choice in stage 5.
