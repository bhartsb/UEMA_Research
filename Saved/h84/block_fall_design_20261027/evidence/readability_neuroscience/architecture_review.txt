1. USEFUL SCIENCE AND ITS LIMITS
Adult humans: Rainville et al. (1997) changed reported pain unpleasantness with hypnotic suggestions without changing perceived intensity. This supports separating sensation intensity from negative valuation, not choosing a numerical robot pain scale. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9252330/

Mice: Corder et al. (2019) manipulated an amygdala ensemble associated with pain unpleasantness. The manipulation changed affective/motivational responses without simply abolishing detection or withdrawal responses. After nerve injury, innocuous touch could recruit this response. Useful inference: detection, aversion, reflexive behavior and current capacity are distinct functions. Do not claim pain universally numbs sensation or that UEMA needs an artificial amygdala. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30655440/ ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6450685/

Adult humans: Seymour et al. (2004) studied higher-order Pavlovian pain conditioning and reported signals consistent with temporal-difference learning. This supports the practical idea that predictors before pain can acquire negative value. It does not demonstrate one-fall motor mastery, identify a complete brain learning algorithm, or validate a particular robot actor-critic. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15190354/ (doi:10.1038/nature02581)

Adult humans: Seymour et al. (2005) studied learning about relief from experimentally induced prolonged pain. Relief had appetitive learning properties. Design inference: an action that ends ongoing pain can become valuable without an extra 'get hurt then heal' reward. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16116445/

Rats: Navratilova et al. (2012) studied postsurgical pain and relief from local anesthetic blockade. Relief supported conditioned place preference and reward-related signaling. This distinguishes relief valuation from restoration of an injured body's function. An analgesic-like change must not silently repair capacity. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3528534/

Reward is not simply one pleasure chemical. Berridge and Robinson (1998) includes primary rat experiments in a broader review: substantial dopamine depletion did not abolish measured taste-affect reactions. Useful software lesson: present pleasant experience, seeking it, and learning its predictor need not be the same variable. Do not relabel TD error 'pleasure' or implement neurotransmitter modules for decoration. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9858756/

Infants: Han and Adolph (2021) observed ordinary falls, rapid return to play and protective body/behavior factors. Many falls had little apparent cost and did not suppress practice. The paper cautions that reactive balance behaviors need not be deliberate impact-minimizing plans. Do not make 'fell' a uniformly large penalty. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8178414/

Infants: Han et al. (2023) followed crawling-to-walking infants on a fall-inducing foam pit. Both avoidance and repeated-entry profiles showed learning in comparisons with individual baselines; repeated entry did not necessarily mean no learning. This supports measuring changed control, exploration, outcomes and retention, not equating learning with never falling. It does not justify ignoring genuinely harmful contacts. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37535540/

3. CONCRETE DESIGN IMPROVEMENTS
A. Keep body-owned physical cause, experienced nociceptive signal, function state and value producer distinct. The actor cannot edit its pain producer, its physical function inventory or the fixed objective weights. A virtual physics rule determines what pressure does to the virtual body; it does not choose a crouch, step, grasp or recovery action.

B. Brief severe events need an event penalty that survives temporal averaging. Retain proposed r = dt*(C - D - eta*E) - lambda_I*deltaA. Label all constants engineering choices. The illustrative 50-ms example with C=0,D=1,E=0,deltaA=.5,lambda_I=1 gives -.55 instead of -.05. It illustrates the arithmetic, not a calibrated pain response. Charge increments in an event's severity envelope, not the full peak at every physics substep. Maintain event identity across contact chatter. Sustained pain must still produce ongoing negative utility after the peak stops increasing; otherwise prolonged static pressure becomes costless. Existing persistent exposure states can supply that separate ongoing cost.

C. Preserve absolute deficits as well as transient pain. A highly predictable damaged state must remain undesirable even when its prediction error is almost zero. Low prediction error obtained by impoverishing the senses is not useful ability. Positive value should remain anchored to retained, actual controllability and healthy function, not arbitrary novelty or a model-declared success. The fixed positive weighted deficit inventory in the revised design makes additional measured impairment count instead of hiding it beneath a worse family maximum.

D. Give one real event two teaching routes. Immediate real reward updates the value of the current action. The recorded causal prefix plus ACTUALLY EXECUTED intervening actions teaches the world model to predict later pain/function; real future measurements are targets only. Free rollout from the earlier prefix lets that first sequence train a predictor before a long chain of critic backups has converged. At a later decision, internal imagination can then prefer a different body/gaze/finger continuation. This is a concrete mechanism for rapid revision, not a promise that one gradient update reliably generalizes. Do not invent probabilities for deterministic planner choices or use uncorrected off-policy sequence returns. Preserve rare events, apply replay importance weights and measure update counts/retention.

E. Explain two clocks. A trained feedback policy can correct balance during a movement as new observations arrive. A first novel harmful outcome supplies evidence for changing future predictions and choices. The first response and new learning are not the same thing. Proposed 50-ms decisions and a three-second forecast require measured compute/physics timing; neuroscience alone does not certify them. A sharp event must be captured at the physical sampling timescale rather than averaged away between decisions.

F. Relief: ending a negative cost improves future return and can produce positive TD error relative to an expectation of continued pain. Do not additionally pay an independent positive bonus for restoration after self-induced damage. Compare two trajectories with the same initial and final healthy state: remaining healthy versus deliberately hurting then recovering. The latter must not earn a better return merely from its recovery. Persistent damage must remain recorded even if aversion temporarily falls. This condition also guards pleasant-contact or sensory-gain self-stimulation loops; genuine useful exploration can still have benefit independent of relief.

G. Protect exploration by measuring consequences, not naming behaviors. A harmless tumble or controlled descent is not necessarily a painful fall. A sharp localized impact deserves a strong signal even if it does not end in a fall. Learning should improve useful movement while reducing costly outcomes; a policy that freezes to avoid all experience fails the objective.
